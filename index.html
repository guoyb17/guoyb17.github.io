Research

My research makes performance across population groups a systems design objective, alongside accuracy and resource use. I pursue this agenda across three connected directions.

Hardware–model co-design

My Nature Electronics work revealed how memory capacity and device variability affect performance gaps in compute-in-memory accelerators. Our subsequent FairXbar work explored using hardware non-idealities to reduce these disparities. Together, these findings motivate accelerator design that considers performance across groups alongside aggregate accuracy and resource cost.

Compression for consistent predictions

Quantization and factorization are conventionally used to make models smaller. I repurpose these tools to reduce performance gaps: FairQuantize selectively quantizes weights, while FairLRF sparsifies singular-vector matrices after SVD. Both methods target prediction disparities while preserving overall predictive performance.

Agentic compression and deployment

FairCompressAgent turns user requirements into compression plans and refines them using measured evaluation feedback, targeting FPGA deployment. My systems work also includes deploying compressed models on x86-64, CUDA, ARM64, and Raspberry Pi platforms, and evaluating convolutional and large language models on CPUs and specialized accelerators.
